Introducing ahang-ahang Blog

Launching a personal blog at the intersection of AI-powered productivity, product management, and fatherhood. ahang-ahang means when dad is happy, the kids are happy.

I've been thinking about starting a blog for a while. Today, I'm finally doing it.

This is ahang-ahang — a Korean expression that roughly translates to "when dad is happy, the kids are happy." It captures something I believe deeply: that the energy you bring to your work and your family feeds back into everything else. When I'm productive and fulfilled at work, I'm a better father. When I'm present and playful at home, I'm a sharper thinker at work. The two are not in tension — they amplify each other.

This blog will cover three topics that sit at the center of my daily life.

1. AI Harness: Maximizing Productivity with AI Tools

I work as a Product Manager at LINE Pay Thailand, where I manage payment infrastructure, core system separations, and cross-border integrations. The complexity is real — dozens of stakeholders, regulatory constraints, and technical dependencies that shift weekly.

To stay on top of it, I've been building my own AI harness — a custom pipeline built on Claude Code that automates the tedious parts of my workflow. Spec writing, technical reviews, implementation planning, QA verification — I've wired these into a single orchestrated pipeline that I can trigger from the command line.

This blog itself was created using that harness. I typed one command, and it generated the post, updated the index, and deployed to GitHub Pages. No CMS. No manual file management. Just a Claude Code skill called /blog.

I'll share what I've learned about:

  • Designing effective AI harnesses (the architecture matters more than the prompts)
  • Where AI tools genuinely save time vs. where they create false efficiency
  • The emerging era of personalized harnesses — why off-the-shelf AI tools will give way to custom-built pipelines tailored to individual workflows

2. Product Manager Mindset

Product management is a craft that looks different in every company. At LINE Pay, it means navigating between business stakeholders in Japan and Thailand, engineering teams across multiple squads, and regulatory requirements that change faster than our sprint cycles.

I'll write about the mental models and decision frameworks I use:

  • How to prioritize when everything is urgent and nothing is clear
  • Running effective sprint-based production in a team that's never done it before
  • The art of saying "we decided not to proceed" — and why that's sometimes the best product decision
  • Managing dependencies across core system separations without losing momentum

These won't be abstract management theory. They'll be grounded in real situations — disguised enough to respect confidentiality, specific enough to be useful.

3. ahang-ahang: Reflections on Fatherhood

The name of this blog isn't just a cute phrase. It's a daily practice.

I have young children. The hours between 6 PM and 8 PM are sacred — bath time, stories, the small rituals that build trust. But I also have a demanding job with responsibilities that span time zones. The tension is constant, and there's no clean solution.

What I've found is that the answer isn't "work-life balance" — that framing assumes the two are in competition. The answer is energy management. When I invest in systems that reduce friction at work (like the AI harness), I reclaim cognitive bandwidth for the evening. When I'm fully present at home, I sleep better and think more clearly the next morning.

I'll write occasionally about:

  • Practical systems for protecting family time without career compromise
  • The surprisingly transferable skills between parenting and product management (negotiation, prioritization, staying calm under chaos)
  • What "ahang-ahang" looks like in practice — not as an ideal, but as a messy, ongoing experiment

Why This Blog Exists

Three reasons:

For future collaborators. If you're considering working with me, this blog is a window into how I think. The technical posts show how I approach tools and systems. The PM posts show how I make decisions. The personal posts show what I care about.

For the AI agents. Every post on this blog exists in two formats: an HTML page for human readers and a structured Markdown file optimized for AI agents. When an AI agent searches the web for topics I've written about, the .md files are designed to be easily parsed and cited. This is intentional — I believe the next wave of content discovery will be agent-mediated, and I want this blog to be ready.

For me. Writing forces clarity. The act of structuring a thought into a post reveals gaps in my understanding that I wouldn't have found otherwise. This blog is a thinking tool disguised as a publication.

What's Next

I'll be publishing regularly on all three topics. The cadence will be driven by what I'm actually doing — not by a content calendar. When I build something interesting with the harness, I'll write about it. When a product decision teaches me something, I'll share it. When fatherhood surprises me, I'll reflect on it.

If any of this resonates, welcome to ahang-ahang.

— David
